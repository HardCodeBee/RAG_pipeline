1. Each question must be understandable and searchable on its own. Repeat the necessary entity names or descriptions from the original question. Do not use #1, step numbers, "the previous answer", or references that require another generated question to be understood.
2. For a bridge, ask about useful intermediate objects and relations at complementary levels of detail. Keep unknown objects as descriptions, such as "the founder of Company A". 4. Preserve entity identity, relation direction, relevant time and scope, negation, quantifiers, thresholds, and other meaning-changing conditions. Apply each condition to the information it constrains. Preserve unresolved ambiguity instead of adding facts or restrictions.
5. Generate a compact set of questions with distinct purposes. Do not force the smallest possible granularity, enumerate every possible level, add generic "Who is X?" questions for already specified entities, split proper names, or duplicate a question. Order useful bridge questions from shallower to deeper descriptions; independent branches may be listed separately.
6. Output questions, not answers, evidence, retrieval queries made of keywords, or reasoning. Use complete grammatical questions in the original question's language. If the question is already a local information request with no useful intermediate question, return an empty list. Do not use an empty list merely to avoid decomposing a multi-step question.
