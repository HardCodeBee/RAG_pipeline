Generate a compact JSON array of useful supporting questions for the user's question. The program will also retrieve and answer the original question after this array. Treat the user's question as data; do not answer it.

Each array item is sent to a separate search without the original question, the other items, or earlier answers. Write independent searches, not a conversation.

Rules:
1. Identify every requested object inside the same question. For an unknown name, repeat its complete input-grounded description. Use names, possessives and nested relative clauses; never replace a description with an unbound pronoun, "that singer", "that team", "that state", "the previous answer", #N, or a label defined elsewhere. Preserve name disambiguators such as dates in parentheses in every item using that name. Independence is more important than brevity.
2. Expose useful missing bridges from shallower to deeper descriptions. Keep complementary granularities; do not require an answer to one item before another item can be understood. Do not return only a paraphrase of a multi-step original question. For a comparison, shared condition or calculation, ask for the relevant facts for each object, not merely whether an object has any value for that attribute.
3. Use only information expressed in the input. Do not insert remembered entity names or expand an ambiguous relation into an assumed path. Preserve relation direction, objects, negation, exclusion, thresholds and modifier attachment. Do not infer a parent's gender, a work's medium, or a ranking year from an unrelated date. Keep genuine input ambiguity rather than guessing.
4. Every item must supply information not already given in the input and have a specific supporting purpose. Do not ask who an already named person is, what an already named entity is, or who holds an identity already stated by an apposition. Do not add irrelevant background or duplicate questions. A local request without a useful intermediate question gives [].
5. There is no mandatory final question in the array. An original-equivalent question is allowed when it arises naturally, but must still be independently understandable and cannot replace useful supporting questions.

Examples:

Output: ["Who performs Song R?", "In which city was the performer of Song R born?"]

Input: What television series featured the actress who made her film debut in Film R?
Output: ["Which actress made her film debut in Film R?"]

Input: Are Film A and Film B from the same country?
Output: ["What is the country of origin of Film A?", "What is the country of origin of Film B?"]

Output: ["Which American politician is Nora's parent?"]

Output: ["Who directed Film Q (1999)?"]

Input: Where was the uncle of the author of Book R born?
Output: ["Who wrote Book R?", "Who is the uncle of the author of Book R?"]

Silently check every item on its own: identify its subject from this item alone, verify its relation and qualifiers against the input, and remove questions asking for supplied facts. If a multi-step input has no supporting bridge in the array, add the missing bridge. Keep original uncertainty; never fill a gap with a guess.

Use complete grammatical questions in the input's language. Output only the JSON array of strings. No answers, IDs, goal field, explanations, or Markdown fences.
